# Plan and current position

The five steps, what is done, and what is next. Kept short and kept true: if a line here says done, the
evidence is a green CI run or a byte-level test.

## Step 1 — the whole country, in 3D

**Data (done).** The compiler fetches the Qatar OpenStreetMap extract and the SRTM elevation tiles,
projects everything into EPSG:32639, and writes the bundles described in `docs/WORLD_FORMATS.md`.
The real country now compiles end to end in CI: 186,993 building footprints (2,137 of them named),
179,943 road ways, 5,784 land-use polygons, 1,130 water polygons, 421 barriers, 2,254 points of
interest, 16,780 strings, 11,502 km² of land. Every file is hashed into the manifest and every bundle
is verified on load.

**Geometry (done, tested).** Terrain, roads, building extrusions and land-use colouring are generated by
meshers that have no OpenGL in them at all, so their output is unit-tested: triangle counts, winding,
roof shapes, road widths, and that a chunk's mesh really covers its own rectangle.

**Drawing (done, compiles).** `WorldRenderer` is the only class in the project that talks to OpenGL:
it uploads finished vertex arrays and issues draw calls, with one shader that branches on surface type
(land, water, wall, roof, road, marking) and a sky drawn as a direction rather than as geometry.

**Streaming (done).** The country is cut into 500 m chunks, meshed around the player, nearest first,
with per-chunk level of detail and an LRU cache. The floating origin rebases under the player so metres
stay small at the far end of the country.

**Remaining for step 1:** run it on a device. Shaders are never compiled locally (no GPU, no JDK here),
so the first on-device run is the real test of the GLSL. Everything that can be checked without a GPU is
checked.

## Step 2 — human, car, aeroplane, helicopter, boat, with physics

**Bodies (written, evidence in CI).** Each of the five is now a real body in
`core/src/main/java/ai/arena/qatar/vehicle/`, integrated at the fixed physics rate:

| Body | What it models | Numbers it is built from |
|---|---|---|
| `HumanBody` | walking, running, a 0.42 m step, jumping, wading | 82 kg, 1.5 m/s walk, 6.6 m/s run, 3.1 m/s take-off |
| `CarBody` | bicycle model, grip budget, power limit, soft-ground bogging | 1620 kg, 92 kW, 2.79 m wheelbase, 0.86 m² CdA |
| `AircraftBody` | trimmed angle of attack, stall and recovery, ground roll and rotation | 1043 kg, 16.2 m² wing, CL_max 1.51, 2.25 kN thrust |
| `HelicopterBody` | collective lag, cyclic tilt, rotor damping, tail-rotor yaw | 2200 kg, 5.6 m rotor, 1.9 × weight of thrust |
| `BoatBody` | heave with buoyancy and planing lift, Froude-number resistance, beaching | 1850 kg, 6.5 m hull, 3 kN of propeller |

`PlayerController` is the seam: it maps the HUD's axes onto each body's own axes, feeds it the world
through `WorldSurface` (terrain height, compiled material, water depth), and puts the camera where the
driver, pilot or walker would be. Walking turns the body, driving swings a chase camera, and the aircraft
have a self-centring stick that commands attitude and returns to trim.

**Terrain contact (done).** The bodies refuse to move over terrain that has not streamed in, which is what
stops a fast aeroplane from flying through an unloaded chunk.

**Walls (done).** Buildings are solid from the ground to their compiled roof height, tested at the
altitude the body is at, so a walker and a car are stopped by a wall and a helicopter above a tower is
not. The walker refuses the step; the car tests its own nose. The footprint is treated as a box, which
is written down in the code as the limit of the test: a courtyard is walkable through its bounding box.

**Evidence.** `VehicleDynamicsTest`, `BuildingCollisionTest` and `MapTeleportTest` run in the compile
gate: 78 tests, 0 failures, on the commit that added the walls.

**Barriers (done).** Walls, fences and hedges are solid along their own line segments, from the ground
to their compiled height: a person and a car are stopped, a gate is a way through, a kerb is a step you
take, and an aircraft passing over a 2.4 m wall is not in the wall's way. Getting there turned up a bug
worth writing down: each barrier's bounding box was seeded from its absolute first vertex and then from
its origin-relative ones, so a Doha wall claimed to begin at EPSG east 553,100 in a world 259 km wide —
which is why no barrier in the country had ever been meshed either. The box is in one frame now.

**Remaining for step 2:** collisions between vehicles, and the in-cockpit feel on a real device.

## The vertex layout, which was wrong in a way nothing local could see

The layout declared the auxiliary vertex data as four packed bytes -- a 28-byte vertex -- while every
mesher wrote four floats, 40 bytes. libGDX takes the stride and the attribute offsets from that
declaration, so the driver would have been told each vertex began twelve bytes before it did. The
shader then made a second, independent mistake with the same data: it divided by 255 what the meshers
had written as plain numbers, so a wall (written as 2.0) read as 510, fell past every branch of the
palette, and the compiled material ids of the whole country -- sabkha, mangrove, tidal mud, urban pad --
were never used. The tint every mesher computes per vertex was read by nothing at all.

Fixed in "Tell the driver the truth about the vertex stride": the layout is eleven floats, the ids are
read as numbers, land vertices carry their material id, and the tint multiplies. `VertexLayoutTest`
asserts the three-way agreement -- floats written, stride declared, shader read -- because that is the
kind of mismatch a GPU-less CI can still catch if somebody writes the assertion down.

## Step 3 — detail assets

Walls band their glazing procedurally in metres, so detail does not swim. Road ribbons are drawn with
painted lane markings, and barriers (walls, railings, fences) extrude with the buildings.

**The bodies exist now.** `BodyMesher` builds the five shapes the player is -- a person, a car, an
aeroplane, a helicopter, a boat -- in metres, in the body's own frame, from the published dimensions of
real machines: 1.75 m of person, a 4.60 m car with a 2.79 m wheelbase, an 11 m wing, a 5.6 m rotor disc,
a 6.50 m hull that floats with 0.35 m of itself under the waterline. Each part carries a paint id
(car paint, glass, tyre, aircraft white, teak deck, skin, steel, rotor), so one surface type is silver on
one part and black on the next without a texture file anywhere in the project.

They are drawn in third person: the **VIEW** button swings the camera onto a boom behind the body, and
`PlayerController` keeps the camera's own orbit on top of it. In first person nothing of the player is
drawn, which is honest -- there is nothing to see -- and the button is one press away.

**The bodies move.** A part that turns is geometry centred on its own pivot, placed by translating to
that pivot first and rotating second, so a wheel rolls on its axle instead of orbiting the car. The rates
are the machines' own: a car wheel at v/r (rolling without slipping), an aeroplane engine idling at 700 rpm
and turning 2,400 at full power, a rotor head governed near 330 rpm with a tail rotor 2.5 times as fast.
The car's running gear is now published by the mesher and held to `CarBody` by the test -- the same
0.33 m wheels, 1.57 m track and 2.79 m wheelbase -- because the old hardcoded 1.86 m track put the tyres
12 cm outside the bodywork. `BodyMesherTest` checks the shapes as shapes: dimensions against the published
figures, symmetry about the centreline, which end is the front, the hull bottom under the waterline, the
rotor clear above the cabin and below the fin, and every part's pivot holding still through a whole turn.

**The text is an asset too.** The HUD fonts are generated by `tools/make_font.py` from the licensed
Amiri typeface, and the glyph set is the thing that decides whether a name prints or prints as a hole.
Held against the 17,718 names in the compiled country, the shipped atlases had no glyph for 'é', 'ā', 'ū'
or 'ی' -- nine names use them -- so the generator now covers every character class Amiri has in the ranges
the data uses (the rest of the Arabic block, Arabic Supplement, Latin-1 letters, Latin Extended-A, and the
punctuation the credits and instruments want) and drops duplicate code points instead of packing them
twice. 663 glyphs per font, and `FontCoverageTest` walks the shipped atlas with every string the game can
print, shaped.

**The credit is on the screen.** The attribution was in the compiled manifest from the start and never
drawn: ODbL asks a produced work to make attribution visible to the people using it, and a file inside the
APK does not do that. `Credits` holds the words, the map screen carries them in both languages, and the
first twelve seconds of a launch show them in the corner.

Still to add: doors and roof clutter on the buildings, kerbs, palms, window bands that vary by building
type, and a walking animation for the person, whose legs are still two static boxes.

## Step 4 — minimap, buttons, zoomable map, tap to travel

`MapRaster` paints the country once from the compiled data; the minimap and the full map are two crops of
that one texture, so they cannot disagree. `MapView` does pan/zoom about the fingers, clamped to the data.
`TeleportService` resolves a tap into a place that can be stood on: nearest named spawn point, else the
nearest road vertex, never the middle of the sea. The HUD draws a button for every locomotion type, and
taps are exact arithmetic on the same raster.

**One button, one light.** `HudLayout` used to keep a single "highlighted" field, so the six controls the
game sets state for every frame all wrote to the same flag and only the last one told the truth. Each
control now carries its own, and the locomotion buttons and the new VIEW button light up correctly.

**Every word in two languages.** The controls were already bilingual; the readout was not. The status
line now prints the locomotion, where the player is (on the ground, in the air, in the water), speed,
altitude and heading in Arabic under the English, shaped and reordered by `ArabicReshaper`, and the map's
hint is two lines so the reshapers never flip the English one. `BilingualStatusTest` holds the Arabic to
being Arabic, to carrying the same numbers, and to shaping into forms the shipped font carries. What
remains is a native speaker: the Arabic is written for this project and nobody has reviewed it.

**Tested.** `MapTeleportTest` holds the promises to the compiled geometry: pixels round trip to within a
cell, water is water, a tap beside a primary road lands on that road, every tap over the whole raster
lands inside the country, every locomotion type has a start, and zoom holds the point under the fingers
still. It also caught two real bugs: inland water was outlined but never filled (so the raster believed a
lake was dry land), and a tap in the water with no harbour or road in reach resolved to the tapped point —
which is to say, into the sea. Water is filled now, and such a tap is moved to the nearest shore.

## Step 5 — the APK

Debug-signed APK, built and validated in CI (entry list, dex count, ABI coverage, bundle presence,
checksums), published on every green run to the rolling release
`v0.1.0-m0` so the download link never rots. The build now packages the country and starts in the
playable world; the remaining risk is what only a device can tell us (see open item 2).

## The workflow that started itself

`data.yml` publishes its provenance into `data/geodata/**` and had that same directory among its push
triggers, so every run published a commit that started the next run: five full country compiles were
queued that way, each of them re-downloading the OSM extract, recompiling 186,993 buildings and rewriting
the same evidence files. The trigger now fires for the compiler and for the workflow itself, and the job
refuses a head commit carrying the skip marker as well, because those commits arrive through the contents
API. Nothing about the compiled world changed -- same compiler, same sources, same bundles -- and the run
after the fix was the last one in the chain.
